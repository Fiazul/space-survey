# Galactic Core

Ctrl+P → **Milky Way Galactic Core** offers Sagittarius A* at 7.9 AU, a 2 AU close view, a polar view, 79 AU, and a 500 ly overview. Search `Sagittarius`, `black hole`, or `galactic core`. Arrivals face the hole with a circular velocity from the same pseudo-Newtonian force used in flight. Black hole systems bypass the legacy 10,000 km/s limiter so inspection velocities remain orbital; ordinary systems retain their current behavior. Normal system travel, Tab targeting, returning to Sol, and anchored saves use the existing paths.

The destination is at J2000 RA 266.416837°, Dec −29.007811°, 26,673 ly from Sol. The local system frame remains in kilometres; the galactic offset never enters the ship's anchored state. The core samples retain 3D positions in light-years and project to the existing depth-tested sky shell, with real angular size and parallax as the observer moves. A seeded bulge, nuclear disc and central cusp contain 30,000 representative stars; twelve central stars are physical, targetable bodies using the shared stellar recipes. These samples represent a dense population, not a complete measured census or all the stars in the Milky Way.

Sagittarius A* uses 4.30 million solar masses and spin a = 0.9, prograde with the flow (angular momentum along scene −Y). `BlackHoleRecipe` derives every length from r_g = GM/c² ≈ 6.35 million km: Kerr outer horizon r₊ = r_g(1+√(1−a²)) ≈ 1.436 r_g (9.12 million km, the body radius used for horizon capture), Bardeen prograde ISCO ≈ 2.321 r_g, prograde/retrograde equatorial photon orbits ≈ 1.558/3.910 r_g, marginally bound orbit ≈ 1.732 r_g, and horizon angular velocity a·c/(2r₊). The model flow runs from the ISCO to 36 r_g (the same physical size as the earlier 18 Schwarzschild radii). Null rays integrate the Kerr metric in Cartesian Kerr-Schild form (Hamiltonian, RK4, step ∝ distance from the horizon, at most 96 steps per pixel), traced as forward rays of the time-reversed hole so the coordinates stay regular across the horizon. Because spin breaks the orbital-plane symmetry the trace is fully 3D, so the shadow comes out lopsided: edge-on it is flattened on the approaching side at impact parameter 2.84 r_g and reaches 6.83 r_g on the receding side. `test_black_hole_views` checks both edges against the closed form. Disk-plane crossings create direct and higher-order images. Each crossing uses the redshift g of a circular equatorial Kerr emitter (Ω = c³/(GM(r^{3/2}+a)), frame dragging included), times the static-observer blueshift at the camera. Brightness scales as g⁴: I_ν/ν³ is invariant, so frequency-integrated intensity carries g⁴. Colour shifts with g, so the bright, bluer approaching side comes from the trace and is not painted on. Weak-field paths outside 46 r_g are approximated as straight. Full GRMHD, radiative transfer, light-travel delays and relativistic ship motion are not modeled. Ship gravity is covered in the pseudo-Newtonian paragraph below.

**Sensor view, time-lapse ×40.** Real time stays in force for gravity, plunge and horizon capture, HUD warnings, lensing, the shadow, Doppler beaming and redshift. Only the plasma display runs on one sensor clock, `BlackHoleRenderer.TIME_LAPSE` = 40 × the shared simulation clock, so time warp still stacks: the disk flow, hotspots, flares and turbulence. The HUD shows `SENSOR VIEW · TIME-LAPSE ×40` while the hole is on screen. A single factor keeps Kepler ratios real: every radius turns at ω = c³/(GM(r^{3/2}+a)) × 40, so inner flow laps outer flow exactly as in Kerr. Two targets set N. The true ISCO lap is ~590 s (2π(r^{3/2}+a)·GM/c³), and it must stay at least 8 s on screen so the inner ring never strobes, which requires N ≤ 73. The outer flow filling the view from ~1.2 AU moves ~1.1·N px per 2.5 s at 720p, and visible motion within 2–3 s needs N ≳ 10. N = 40 meets both: the ISCO lap is 14.8 s on screen, hotspot laps run 18–45 s, and from 1.2 AU ~74% of flow pixels change within 2.5 s. The flow texture is a flow map. Two noise phases, half a 160 sensor-second cycle apart, are each advected at the local Kerr rate, crossfaded, and restarted on a fresh noise offset while invisible. Differential rotation therefore never winds the pattern into ever-tighter, aliasing spirals: a 60 s capture from 1.2 AU stays coherent, and fixed-camera frame differences grow smoothly with time.

Four seeded hotspots are compact plasmoids modeled on GRAVITY's 2018 orbiting-hotspot fits. Each is born between 1.2 and 2.1 ISCO radii, orbits at its own Kerr rate (inner spots lap outer ones), and fades after 15–30 sensor minutes. Behind each trails a synchrotron-cooling wake: 150 s, the NIR cooling time for B ≈ 100 G, so a single frame shows the direction of flow. Spots flicker on a 6·GM/c³ scale. They pass through the same g⁴ beaming, so they blaze on the approaching side and fade on the receding side. Flares follow the observed Sgr A* NIR rate, several a day (one per 6 sensor hours, so ~9 minutes on screen). The first is scheduled 75–135 s after each arrival. Each rises in 3 sensor minutes and decays over ~15 minutes, with variability on 3 and 12 GM/c³ (GM/c³ ≈ 21 s), heating the hotspots (~3–8× brighter) and the innermost flow. Schedules are deterministic functions of the recipe seed and sensor time (`BlackHoleRenderer.hotspots` / `flare_level`). Small eddies drift radially over ~10 GM/c³ within each flow-map phase; they are procedural turbulence rather than a hydrodynamic simulation.

**Palette.** The physical parts are the shape of the zero-torque thin-disk temperature profile, T ∝ r^{-3/4}(1−√(r_ISCO/r))^{1/4} (Shakura & Sunyaev 1973; Page & Thorne 1974, peaking at 49/36 r_ISCO), and the shift of each fragment's colour temperature to T·g by the traced redshift factor. The sensor-view parts are the absolute scale (peak 7,500 K display colour; real Sgr A* is a radio/NIR synchrotron source, not a thermal optical disk), a warm-biased blackbody ramp (Helland's fit to the blackbody locus), and the emissivity profile and amplitudes. The result is a white-hot inner edge, a golden mid-disk and a deeper orange rim. The approaching side reads blue-white and the receding side deeper orange because of g, not per-side painting; `test_black_hole_views` checks that the approaching side is whiter. Hotspots use 2× the peak, and flares raise the temperature by up to 35%, so they flash white-hot. The flow saturates at 60% display brightness with its hue preserved, so hotspots stay the brightest features. Background sky through the plasma is the lensed background only, at 4% transmission per crossing; the alpha fade to the ordinary sky starts outside the flow, so no straight-through star is drawn over it.

Representative stars share an enhanced exposure so their unresolved light adds into the distant bulge. This is a display recipe, not a claim that Sagittarius A* is a visibly bright quasar. The scan panel and arrival toast identify the enhancement. Radius and gravity stay physical, and no photosphere or planet is invented for the hole. Close approaches show strong-gravity and event-horizon telemetry. Crossing the physical horizon now opens **Humano**: the intact ship coasts through five seconds of cubic mist, a one-second loading card covers the swap, and the space view disappears for a third-person dinosaur playing a human endless runner on an arcade screen. Space, Up, click or the Jump button starts/jumps; a collision offers a restart. Esc or Return to ship restores a circular orbit at exactly 2 AU, preserving hull and progress. This interior is a fictional easter egg; exterior vacuum and gravity keep their existing behavior. A fictional **0.6 AU no-escape approach** now forces radial infall at any speed, including Ctrl+D no-death. The floor speed covers the capture-boundary-to-horizon distance in four simulation seconds; faster inbound motion is retained. Swept crossings enter on the near side, so a large physics step cannot skip the region. The HUD reads **CORE CAPTURE — NO ESCAPE**. The direct Humano preview works with no-death too. Ctrl+P → search `Humano` → **through the horizon → Humano** is a direct preview entry. Saves made during the arcade also store the safe 2 AU orbit.

Core stars use one point draw and gas uses one batched draw. Twelve physical stars keep ordinary mesh/impostor LOD. The hole traces only within its projected bounds and disappears below the subpixel threshold. While visible in front of the camera, a 1024×512 all-sky background viewport projects the same physical core positions: transparent gas is included and the ship/UI are excluded. It covers escaping rays in every direction, including behind the observer. This background is cached and updates only after observer movement exceeding 10⁻⁸ ly; turning the camera does not redraw it. The background viewport suspends at unresolved distances and on return to ordinary systems. The original outside-in galaxy GLB remains hidden, and the local-Sol catalogue backdrop is hidden in the core.

Close views cover the whole camera when the flow subtends a large angle, including outward views from inside the flow and camera roll. Far views keep bounded geometry and subpixel culling. Core batches draw before the lens; foreground transparent exhaust draws after it. The exhaust's screen refraction is skipped in black-hole systems because Godot's opaque screen copy precedes lens compositing; the emitting plume remains visible. The plasma uses the shared celestial simulation clock, so time warp advances both flight and flow. With a = 0.9 the innermost flow completes an orbit in about 9.8 minutes of real time (14.8 s in the ×40 sensor view); the galactic bulge itself has much longer orbital periods.

Ship gravity near the hole is pseudo-Newtonian (`scripts/flight/black_hole_gravity.gd`): F = GM / (r^(2−β) (r − r_H)^β), β = r_ISCO/r_H − 1 ([Artemova, Björnsson & Novikov 1996, ApJ 461, 565](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1996ApJ...461..565A)). It puts the innermost stable circular orbit exactly at Kerr's (2.32 r_g prograde at a = 0.9), diverges exactly at the horizon, reproduces the marginally bound orbit within 2 % (1.70 vs 1.73 r_g), and is Paczyński–Wiita at a = 0. Spin acts through the ship's orbit as a·cos i about the disk axis (−Y): exact for prograde/retrograde equatorial orbits, interpolated between. Frame dragging (Lense–Thirring precession) is not modeled; the force stays central. Ordinary bodies and Sol keep plain inverse-square. Arrivals, F6 circularize and arcade return use the same force, so their orbits stay circular. Near the hole each integrator substep covers at most 0.005 rad of local orbit and time warp is lowered until a frame fits 128 substeps; circular orbits outside the capture region at 20 r_g are checked over five orbits at 60 and 20 fps. A start at 0.95 ISCO is already within the gameplay capture region and proceeds directly inward. The force model retains the physical orbit analysis (energy and angular momentum against the potential barrier): **ORBIT DECAYING — BELOW STABLE ORBIT** inside the ISCO; **PLUNGE — BURN Δv TO ESCAPE**, or **NO ESCAPE AT CURRENT THRUST** when boosted thrust held to the horizon gives less Δv than the cheapest single burn that really escapes (searched over in-plane headings, each candidate re-checked with the full plunge test, +2 % margin; cancelling the fall alone leaves no angular momentum and still plunges, so a braked ship 30 r_g out needs ~24,000 km/s, not its inbound speed); **INSIDE PHOTON ORBIT**; and the ship's clock rate dτ/dt from the Kerr equatorial metric, once below 0.99. These calculations describe the physical model. The live HUD instead warns **PLUNGE — CAPTURE AHEAD AT 0.6 AU** for an approaching plunge and **CORE CAPTURE — NO ESCAPE** inside the gameplay boundary, rather than offering a physical escape burn that might enter that boundary. Three feel cues follow the same state but never act on it. A drone plays the local Kerr orbital frequency × 2¹⁷ (about 19 Hz at six ISCO radii, 222 Hz at the ISCO, 376 Hz at the horizon), louder closer, silent beyond six ISCO radii. A camera-only low-frequency shake starts inside three ISCO radii and is strongest in a plunge. Inside 0.6 AU, the explicit gameplay capture described above replaces orbital integration and overrides thrust/steering; outside it, this force model remains in use.
